1Background

Clinical educator interpretation and assessment of student work is known to differ widely across individuals. A shift to programmatic assessment has heightened the impact of individual professional judgment on student progress.

This pilot applied an assessment literacy and alignment process within the DDS2 Cons Dent simulation component to reduce the influence individual assessors have on student outcomes.

2Aims & Objectives
Primary Aim

Identify areas of difference among Cons Dent educators and facilitate discussion to develop a shared understanding of the expected standard.

Secondary Aim

Help educators understand how their professional interpretation of the assessment checklist influences student progress, supporting shared standard-setting.

6Results
Key Finding

Across 10 typodent stations and 11 educators, 2,933 flagged assessor-pair disagreements were recorded. All 21 checklist items generated disagreements. 29% spanned ≥3 levels — substantive, not marginal divergence.

Flagged pairs · checklist item × station (top 10 items)

Darker cells = more flagged assessor pairs. White = no disagreements recorded for that station.

MC20 generated the most disagreements (251 pairs, present across all 10 stations), indicating a systemically contested checklist item.

MC19 showed the highest average score difference (2.67), with a peak of 30 flagged pairs in Station 1 alone.

Structured discussion led to a softening of overly lenient or strict judgements, with preliminary data showing improved grading consistency.

"The critical discussion of these items identified underlying lenient and strict tendencies, promoting open debate and exploration of their own standards."

7Discussion & Future Directions

This pilot with 11 educators across 10 stations demonstrates that structured standard-setting exercises can surface and reduce significant inter-rater variability before it impacts student outcomes. Formal evaluation will conclude in mid-June 2026, with expansion to the whole MDS simulation program planned.

1Background

Clinical educator interpretation and assessment of student work is known to differ widely. A shift to programmatic assessment has heightened the impact of individual professional judgment on student progress.

This pilot applied an assessment literacy and alignment process within the DDS2 Cons Dent simulation component to reduce the influence individual assessors have on student outcomes.

2Aims & Objectives
Primary Aim

Identify areas of difference among Cons Dent simulation educators and facilitate discussion to develop a shared understanding of the expected standard.

Secondary Aim

Help clinical educators understand how their own professional interpretation of the checklist could influence student progress, supporting shared standard-setting.

5Results
Key Discrepancies Identified

Across 10 typodent stations and 11 educators, 2,933 flagged assessor-pair disagreements were recorded. All 21 checklist items generated disagreements at ≥2 levels.

Excessively deep preparations — educators held divergent views on severity. MC20 generated the highest total (251 flagged pairs across all 10 stations), indicating a systemically contested item.

Residual caries near the dentinoenamel junction (DEJ) — MC19 showed the highest average score difference (2.67), reflecting wide individual variation in threshold expectations.

29% of disagreements spanned ≥3 levels (3- or 4-level difference), indicating not merely marginal but substantive divergence between some educator pairs.

Structured discussion promoted open exploration of individual standards, leading to a softening of overly lenient or overly critical judgements and convergence toward a shared benchmark.

"Preliminary data reveals an improvement in alignment and consistency in grading across all stations."

6Discussion

This pilot with 11 educators across 10 stations demonstrates that structured standard-setting exercises can surface and reduce significant inter-rater variability before it impacts student outcomes. The breadth of disagreements — spanning all 21 items — underscores that variance is not confined to a few ambiguous criteria.

Formal evaluation will be completed at the end of the component in mid-June 2026. The approach will be expanded to incorporate the whole simulation program at Melbourne Dental School.
